Published on LinkedIn Platform breakdown

Your LinkedIn ranking just changed, and most creators haven’t noticed.
(Favikon’s CEO emailed me the breakdown on Monday.)

The biggest shift: one viral post won’t carry you anymore.

The score is now built from four parts:

→ Reach (30%): how many people see your typical post, based on your median over the last 90 days
→ Engagement (30%): likes, comments and shares on an average post
→ Audience (30%): how many people follow you
→ Consistency (10%): 25 posts in 90 days earns the full 10%

On LinkedIn, the total is then multiplied by an authenticity rating, from ×0.50 to ×1.15.

I already publish 3 times a week, so consistency is maxed out. My gains have to come from elsewhere.

So I’m adjusting four habits:

1. Tracking my median impressions every Friday instead of celebrating my best one
2. Answering replies properly, since conversation depth now counts
3. Dropping comment swaps, so the same names don’t fill every thread
4. Asking one question before anything goes out: could only I have written this?

Favikon now rewards the creator you are on an average Tuesday.

That’s the version your clients meet too.

02 B2B AI SaaS: Trial-to-Paid Conversion AI / SaaS · Anonymized 0.9% → 6.3% ▾

Problem

Trial-to-paid sat at 0.9%. The founder thought the pricing page needed a redesign. The real issue was positioning: the landing page spoke to everyone in the category, so the people the product served best couldn't see it was for them. Onboarding had the same gap. It explained features instead of confirming the user had made the right call.

What I did

I talked to customers who had converted, stayed and referred others. What they understood about the product, other trial users didn't. We narrowed the positioning around that person and rewrote the headline, hero section, onboarding emails and in-app tooltips in their language.

Result

Trial-to-paid went from 0.9% to 6.3% in 11 weeks.

How I think about this work

01

Positioning before content

More posts don't fix a message aimed at the wrong person. I find the positioning problem first. Content built on top of it just repeats the mistake faster.

02

Specific over comfortable

"Increased engagement" is a number nobody can check. A conversion rate, a retention rate or an impression count with a date attached is one you can. I use the second kind.

03

Content does the sales call first

Every post shows a buyer how you think. When they're ready, they skip the other options and come straight to you. I build content for that day, not for likes.
