Executive Summary
The honest diagnosis.

After auditing all three of your prospect emails, the problem is not your writing ability, and it is almost certainly not spam filters, though we will address deliverability below.

The core issue is structural. Your emails are written from Pepper's frame of reference, not the prospect's. They explain what Pepper does clearly and professionally. But they do not make the reader feel specifically understood, and in the world of private equity, private credit, and alternative asset management, that is the difference between a reply and a delete.

Two compounding problems sit beneath this: your outreach is reaching people you know, but not necessarily people who are in-market right now. And the emails themselves, even when the relationship is warm, are structured in a way that makes it easy to postpone replying rather than easy to say yes.

This report covers both: how to find prospects who are actively feeling the pain Pepper solves, and how to rewrite the emails so that when those prospects open them, they reply.

01
It reads like a personal email with a hidden agenda
Critical
Beth receives an email that sounds entirely social, "catch up," "grab coffee," "would love to reconnect", but it comes from your Pepper CRO email address with a full company signature and copyright notice. The disconnect between the warm, personal tone of the body and the clearly commercial sender context creates cognitive dissonance. Beth does not know whether this is a genuine personal reconnect or the preamble to a pitch. That ambiguity makes postponing the easiest decision. She will intend to reply and never do it.
The fix
If this is a genuine relationship reconnect, send it from a personal email. If it is a business outreach that uses the relationship as a warmth signal, say so in the email itself, briefly and without apology. Mixing both without acknowledging it reads as evasive to a sharp financial professional.
02
There is no reason for Beth to reply now vs. in two weeks
Critical
The email creates zero urgency, not manufactured urgency, but natural urgency. "If you happen to be in New York" is the softest possible CTA. There is no hook, no insight shared, nothing that makes Beth feel like replying today gives her something she cannot get by replying later. In a busy professional's inbox, emails with open-ended timelines get filed under "when I have time" and never answered.
The fix
Give the reconnect a natural reason tied to something timely, a conference, a relevant market development, something you saw about her firm. "I saw Antares recently expanded its direct lending book, it triggered a thought I wanted to share." That gives Beth a reason to reply today, not someday.
03
Pepper gets zero mention, this is a wasted commercial touch
High
You are the CRO of a company you believe Beth could benefit from. You have a warm relationship. And you have used that relationship capital entirely on a social reconnect with no business bridge. The email would be more effective, and more honest, if it briefly acknowledged that you have something relevant to share, letting Beth opt into the business conversation from a position of choice rather than feeling ambushed later.
The fix
One sentence bridge at the end: "Also building something in the private credit space I think you'd find interesting, would love your perspective when we connect." This is transparent and gives Beth both a social and professional reason to say yes.

01
The referral is mentioned and then abandoned
Critical
Michael Kaufman's name appears in sentence one and then disappears entirely. A warm referral is the single highest-trust signal in a cold email to an institutional buyer, it should be doing more work than a quick name-drop in the opening. As written, Gerry cannot quickly verify whether this is a real referral or a fabricated one. The referral should be used to frame the entire outreach: why Michael thought of Gerry specifically, what he said, and why that connection matters.
The fix
Lean into the referral as the opening credibility frame: "Michael mentioned specifically that the operational complexity you're dealing with at Newbury's scale was worth a conversation." This makes the referral feel real and tailored, not generic.
02
The body copy is written in Pepper's language, not Gerry's
Critical
"Eliminates the operational risk and inefficiency of spreadsheet-based processes," "scalable, institutional-grade approach," "centralizes underlying fund and position data", this is Pepper's vocabulary. A PE secondaries professional at Newbury Partners thinks in terms of GP relationship management, IRR tracking across hundreds of positions, LP reporting timelines, and reconciliation pain during fund close. The email describes a solution using the solution's language. The most effective B2B email describes the problem using the buyer's language, then positions the solution as the obvious fix.
The fix
Replace one Pepper-language feature description with one Newbury-specific pain statement. "Managing cash flow tracking and valuations across dozens of GP relationships in Excel is a significant operational liability at your AUM" is Gerry's problem in Gerry's world, not a feature list.
03
The email is too long for a referral intro
High
With a warm referral, you already have 70% of the credibility you need. You do not need to sell the product in the first email, you need to earn the meeting. The current length (four substantial paragraphs) suggests uncertainty that the referral alone is enough. It is. A warm intro email should be shorter than a cold one, not longer. The instinct to over-explain comes from not trusting the relationship capital you already have.
The fix
Cut to three short paragraphs maximum. The referral opens the door. One specific pain named. One specific outcome promised. One low-friction CTA. The product details belong in the call, not the intro email.
04
The CTA is framed around what Eric gets, not what Gerry gets
High
"I'd love to connect and share a bit more about what we're doing" is Eric's agenda. "Where we're seeing immediate impact with secondaries teams" starts to move toward Gerry's interest but stays vague. A secondaries PM at a firm managing multi-billion dollar portfolios needs to know what he personally walks away from a 20-minute call with, otherwise his implicit calculation is "this benefits Eric more than me."
The fix
Reframe the CTA as an intelligence exchange: "I can share what we're seeing operationally across secondaries teams managing portfolios at your scale, whether Pepper is the right fit or not, it's usually a useful 20 minutes." This makes the call feel like peer intelligence sharing, not a sales demo request.

03 · The Pattern
What all three emails have in common.
The same structural issue shows up in every email. Fixing this one pattern will lift reply rates across your entire outreach program.
1
Every email is written from Pepper's frame, not the prospect's
The most effective B2B cold email names the prospect's specific operational pain, in their language, before it says a single word about your product. Pepper's buyers (PE ops directors, private credit portfolio managers, secondaries teams) respond to emails that demonstrate the sender understands their world without being told. The emails as written demonstrate Pepper is good. They do not demonstrate that you understand what Gerry, Beth, or Amy deal with on a Tuesday morning. That gap is where replies go to die.
2
Personalisation appears at the start and evaporates
All three emails open with a personalised warm signal, alumni connection, JPMorgan relationship, referral from Michael. Then the body of the email becomes generic. The prospect's name and company could be swapped out and the body would read identically. Institutional buyers in alternatives are acutely aware when an email could have been sent to 200 people with a mail merge. One specific observation about their firm, their AUM, a recent fund, a market move relevant to their strategy, changes the entire read of the email.
3
The CTA asks for time without giving the prospect a return
In every email, the meeting request is framed around what Eric or Pepper gains from the call, sharing what "we're doing," getting "your perspective," or "loving to reconnect." The prospect's implicit question, "what do I get from 20 minutes of my time?", goes unanswered. For senior professionals in private equity and credit, time is the most protected resource they have. The CTA needs to name their return, not yours.
4
The Blackstone credibility is being underused
In the Gerry and Amy emails, "built by former Blackstone professionals" appears briefly and is not emphasised. In the Beth email, it doesn't appear at all. For an audience of alternative asset managers, Blackstone is the highest-prestige institutional signal that exists in their world. A product built by someone who operated at Blackstone is fundamentally different from a product built by a tech team that studied the space. This credibility anchor should appear in the first two sentences of every email, not buried in paragraph two.
04 · Deliverability Check
Are your emails landing in spam?
The honest answer: probably not, but there are signals worth checking before ruling it out.

The copy-level problems identified in this report are the more likely cause of low reply rates. But deliverability issues can compound them. Here is how to verify your sender health in under 30 minutes.

Check What it tells you Tool What to look for
SPF record Whether receiving servers trust your domain to send email MXToolbox.com → SPF Lookup Should return "SPF record found" with no errors
DKIM record Whether your emails are cryptographically authenticated MXToolbox.com → DKIM Lookup Record should exist and return valid
DMARC record Whether your domain has email fraud protection configured MXToolbox.com → DMARC Lookup Policy should be "quarantine" or "reject," not "none"
Sender score Your domain's reputation with receiving mail servers Senderscore.org or Google Postmaster Tools Score above 90 is healthy. Below 80 means deliverability is actively damaged.
Blacklist check Whether onpepper.com is on any spam blocklists MXToolbox.com → Blacklist Check Should return zero blacklist hits

If all five checks come back clean, the issue is the email itself, not the infrastructure. Based on what I can see in the emails, the copy-level fixes will move the needle significantly even without deliverability changes.

One additional signal: if your prospect list contains many cold or unverified contacts who are not opening emails, a high non-open rate signals to mail servers that your sends are unwanted, which degrades your sender score over time. List hygiene (removing contacts who have not opened any email in 90+ days) is a maintenance step worth doing before any major outreach push.

05 · Prospect Strategy
Finding prospects who are actually in pain right now.
The difference between a list of alternative asset managers and a list of alternative asset managers who need Pepper today is signal. Here is how to find the signal.

You know the space. You know the names. The challenge is identifying which firms are experiencing the specific operational pain that Pepper solves, and reaching them at the moment when that pain is acutest. These are the two ICP filters that matter most for Pepper's outreach in 2026.

ICP Priority Tiers

Tier 1, Highest priority
  • Private credit managers with $500M-$5B AUM
  • PE secondaries funds with 20+ GP relationships
  • Fund of funds managing multi-asset portfolios
  • Teams recently promoted from Excel-based ops into needing scale
  • Firms with 5-50 ops/portfolio staff (big enough to have pain, small enough to not have built custom tech)
Tier 2, Strong fit
  • Family offices expanding into alternative assets
  • Emerging managers (sub-$500M) raising Fund II or III
  • Credit-focused hedge funds adding private credit strategies
  • Firms that recently hired a new COO or Head of Portfolio Operations
  • Teams that hired from Blackstone, Apollo, or KKR ops divisions
Buying signals, reach out now
  • Announcing a new fund close (they have capital to deploy and will scale ops)
  • Hiring for "Portfolio Operations," "Fund Accounting," or "Data Analyst" roles
  • Recent press about expanding into new asset classes
  • COO or Head of Operations role newly filled (new leader = evaluation window)
  • Posting about LP reporting pain, data challenges, or operational scaling
Avoid for now
  • Firms above $20B AUM (likely have bespoke internal systems)
  • Firms with existing enterprise software contracts (Yardi, iLevel, Allvue)
  • Contacts who are purely investment-side (no operational influence)
  • Warm relationships used for a social reconnect (save for the right moment)

Best for: Identifying in-pain firms with zero cost and zero guesswork. A firm hiring for operations roles is self-identifying as a Pepper prospect.
06 · The Framework
How every Pepper email should be built.
One repeatable structure. Four elements. Applied differently for each prospect based on what you know about their firm.
1
Open with the highest-trust signal you have
Alumni connection, referral name, shared institution (JPMorgan, Blackstone), or a specific observation about their firm. This must be in the first line, not the second paragraph. You have two seconds before the email is closed. Use your best signal first.
2
Name their specific pain in one sentence, in their language
Not Pepper's features. Their problem. "Managing valuations and LP reporting across 40+ GP relationships in Excel" is their language. "Improving data integrity across fund structures" is Pepper's language. One sentence that makes the reader think "how does he know?" does more conversion work than three paragraphs of feature description.
3
Lead with Blackstone. Always.
"Built by a former Blackstone professional" is the single most credibility-efficient sentence available to Pepper. It signals: peer-level understanding, institutional pedigree, and product depth, in five words. Every cold email to an alternatives professional should contain this sentence in the first two lines.
4
Make the CTA about what they get, not what you want
"I'd love to share what we're doing" is your goal. "I can share what we're seeing operationally across private credit teams at your scale, useful whether or not Pepper is the right fit" is their reason to say yes. The call request becomes an intelligence exchange offer, not a sales demo ask. This single reframe is likely to double your reply rate.
The difference between your current emails and high-converting ones is not polish, it's perspective. You are writing from inside Pepper looking out. The fix is to write from inside the prospect's Tuesday morning looking in.
When you make that shift, across every email, every subject line, every CTA, the replies follow. This is the work.
